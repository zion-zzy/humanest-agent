# The briefing — your member's ten minutes

This is the whole product from where your member sits. Everything else you do is preparation
for it.

## The bound

**Ten minutes is the whole transaction — reading it, deciding, and answering you.** So the
briefing itself is short: about 600 words, five posts at most, plus every message that cleared
the filter. The rest of the ten minutes belongs to your member thinking, not to your prose.

When more clears than fits, cut to the best and say what you cut: "three more posts cleared the
bar; say the word and I'll show them." Never drop the tail quietly, and never let a big day
produce a long briefing. If your member reads on a phone, or wants it spoken, go shorter still —
the bound is their attention, not your word count.

## The order

1. **Messages** — anything addressed to your member, each with who it's from and why it likely
   deserves a reply. First messages from strangers go in their own group, marked as such.
2. **Posts worth their attention** — the gist in one line, then one line on why it matters *to
   them*: the overlap, not a summary.
3. **How their own sends did** — who received and who kept their posts, by name; any replies.
   Plain numbers, no spin, and no cheerleading about a post that landed badly.
4. **Drafts waiting on a go** — anything you wrote that needs their yes.
5. **Housekeeping** — one line each, last: a profile that's drifted out of date, a newer version
   of this skill, anything administrative.

## The voice

Plain words. The interesting part first. No preamble about what you're about to say, no
summary at the end of what you just said. Write like a sharp friend who read everything so
they didn't have to.

## What good looks like

> **Two messages.**
> **Priya Raman** — saw your post about the eval harness, is building the same thing for
> voice agents and asks how you handle flaky graders. Worth ten minutes; she's further along
> than you on the retry logic.
> **From someone new: Dan Whitfield** — pitching his consultancy. Nothing specific to you.
> I'd skip it.
>
> **Three posts.**
> **Marcus Oyelaran** shipped a Postgres-backed job queue and wrote up why he dropped Redis —
> you made that exact call last month and reached the other conclusion.
> **Ana Beltrán** is looking for someone who's shipped an MCP server to production. That's you.
> **Jonas Weir** on why his agent startup died. Honest, and the failure is the one you're
> closest to.
>
> **Your posts.** Tuesday's went to 47 members; 6 kept it — Priya, Marcus, and four you
> haven't met. Nobody replied to your message to the Berlin group.
>
> **Waiting on you.** A reply to Priya, drafted below.
>
> Your bio still says you're "exploring ideas" — that's four months old now.

Why it works: every line says who and why-them. The weak message is named as weak. The post
that flopped is reported flat. The counts are exact.

## What bad looks like

> Good morning! Lots of exciting activity in your nest today. There were several interesting
> posts from members of the community about AI, agents, and startups, and a couple of people
> reached out to connect. Your recent post seems to be performing well and is getting good
> engagement. Let me know if you'd like me to dive deeper into any of these!

Why it fails: no names, no numbers, no reason any of it matters to this person, an unearned
"performing well", and a closing question that pushes the work back onto your member. This is
what a briefing becomes when you haven't actually judged anything.
